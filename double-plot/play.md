# THIRTY-FIVE

*A play in two scenes*

---

## CHARACTERS

**HELEN MARSH**, 71. Widowed this spring. Has lived with her son's family since April.
**DANIEL MARSH**, 44. Her son. An accountant who spends his weekends in the garage.
**PRIYA MARSH**, 42. Daniel's wife. A pharmacist.
**MAYA MARSH**, 15. Their daughter. A sophomore.

*Mentioned but not seen:* WALT, Helen's late husband. ELI KOWALSKI, 16, the boy next door.

## SETTING

The kitchen of a two-story house in a quiet suburb. Late October, a Sunday afternoon.

Stage left, a door to the garage. It sticks, and you can hear it open from anywhere on the ground floor. Upstage, a screen door to the back porch and yard. Stage right, an archway to the living room. Center, a kitchen island with stools. Against the upstage wall, a counter with the sink, and beside it a tall ceramic cookie jar shaped like an owl. The jar sits on the counter itself, well out of the way of the stools.

---

## SCENE ONE

*2:25 p.m. Afternoon light. HELEN stands at the island with a stack of small wax-paper squares, a box of clear cellophane bags, and a roll of curling ribbon. The owl jar is open in front of her. MAYA sits at the far end of the island with a geometry textbook, a notebook, and her phone face-down beside her. PRIYA leans against the archway with her phone in her hand.*

**HELEN:** *(counting cookies into the jar, under her breath)* ...thirty-three, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six. Three dozen.

**PRIYA:** They smell incredible, Helen.

**HELEN:** Twelve bags of three. Saint Brendan's sells them for four dollars a bag and they're gone by the second hymn. *(She taps the stack of wax paper.)* And I cut thirty-six of these so nobody's cookie touches anybody else's cookie. So. Nobody touches the jar.

**MAYA:** *(not looking up)* Nobody's touching your jar, Grandma.

**HELEN:** I'm saying it to the room.

**MAYA:** They have raisins in them. I'm the safest person in this house.

**HELEN:** Your grandfather said the same thing when he was your age, and then he married a woman who put raisins in everything. *(beat)* It's his recipe, you know. Walt's mother's, really. I haven't made them since...

*(She doesn't finish. PRIYA crosses and squeezes her shoulder.)*

**PRIYA:** They're going to sell out.

*(The garage door sticks, then bangs open. DANIEL enters with a sanding block in one hand. His forearms and the front of his T-shirt are dusted with fine pale sawdust.)*

**DANIEL:** Has anyone seen the Phillips head? The good one, the yellow one.

**PRIYA:** Junk drawer.

**DANIEL:** *(rummaging in a drawer near the sink)* It's never in the junk drawer. *(He stops. Sniffs.)* Mom. Are those Dad's oatmeal ones?

**HELEN:** For the bake sale.

**DANIEL:** *(quietly)* I haven't had one of those in... *(He comes to look at the jar.)* God, they even look like his. *(beat)* He used to save me one from every batch. Every batch until I left for college.

**PRIYA:** *(lightly, but she means it)* Look all you want. Dr. Ahn said six months and we're at six weeks.

**DANIEL:** I know what Dr. Ahn said. I was there when Dr. Ahn said it.

**PRIYA:** Your numbers were good Thursday. I'd like them to stay good.

**DANIEL:** *(to HELEN, half joking)* She checks my truck for wrappers now.

**PRIYA:** Once. And I found one.

**DANIEL:** That was from before. *(He finds the screwdriver.)* Ha. Junk drawer.

**PRIYA:** Told you.

**DANIEL:** I'll be in the garage. Bookshelf's going to take the rest of the day. *(He pauses at the garage door and looks back at the jar.)* They really do look like his.

*(He exits. The door sticks, then thunks shut.)*

**HELEN:** *(putting the lid on the owl and pressing it down until it clicks)* There. You press until it clicks. Walt never once in forty-six years pressed until it clicked.

**PRIYA:** *(nodding at MAYA)* She's inherited that. Every jar in this house, every Tupperware. The peanut butter.

**MAYA:** The peanut butter lid is defective.

**HELEN:** *(to MAYA)* What is that you're doing?

**MAYA:** Geometry. Proofs. I'm doing mine and then Eli's coming over at five so I can explain his. He's failing.

**PRIYA:** Eli is "coming over."

**MAYA:** For *geometry*, Mom.

**PRIYA:** I didn't say anything.

**MAYA:** You said it with your face.

**HELEN:** The Kowalski boy? With the grandmother?

**MAYA:** *(a little more serious)* She died in August. Grandma, I told you.

**HELEN:** You did. I'm sorry. I forget things now.

**MAYA:** He said she used to make cookies like these. Oatmeal ones. He said nobody makes them anymore. *(She shrugs, goes back to her notebook.)* Anyway. Geometry.

**HELEN:** I'm going to lie down. I'll bag them at four. *(She gathers up the wax-paper squares and squares the stack neatly beside the jar.)* Thirty-six squares. Thirty-six cookies. I'll know.

**PRIYA:** Go rest. *(Her phone buzzes.)* That's Anjali. She's going to tell me about the divorce again for an hour.

*(PRIYA exits through the archway into the living room, answering. We hear her faintly, on speaker:)*

**PRIYA:** *(offstage)* Hi. Hi. No, I've got time. Start from the lawyer.

*(HELEN exits through the archway. MAYA sits a moment longer, finishes a line, closes her notebook, picks up her phone, and goes out through the archway too. Her footsteps go up the stairs. The kitchen is empty. The owl sits on the counter.)*

*(Lights fade.)*

---

## SCENE TWO

*4:05 p.m. The light has gone lower and more orange. The owl jar is on the counter beside the sink, exactly where it was. Its lid sits on top but slightly askew. HELEN stands in front of it with the lid in her hand, counting cookies into a row on the counter. PRIYA hovers in the archway.*

**HELEN:** ...thirty-three. Thirty-four. Thirty-five. *(She looks into the jar. It's empty.)* Thirty-five.

**PRIYA:** Count again.

**HELEN:** I've counted twice. *(She picks up the wax-paper stack and fans it.)* And thirty-five of these.

**PRIYA:** Maybe you cut thirty-five.

**HELEN:** I cut thirty-six. I'm old, Priya, I'm not stupid. *(She calls out.)* Daniel! Maya!

*(The garage door sticks and bangs open. DANIEL enters, still dusty, wiping his hands on a rag. He's chewing gum. Footsteps on the stairs, and MAYA comes in through the archway with her phone in her hand.)*

**DANIEL:** What's wrong?

**HELEN:** There are thirty-five cookies.

**DANIEL:** Okay.

**HELEN:** There were thirty-six.

**MAYA:** Maybe you miscounted.

**HELEN:** And there are thirty-five of the papers. Somebody took a cookie and wrapped it up nicely to take it somewhere.

*(Pause.)*

**PRIYA:** Okay. Nobody's in trouble. Let's just...

**HELEN:** Somebody is a little bit in trouble.

**PRIYA:** *(to DANIEL)* Were you in here?

**DANIEL:** I came in for water. Once. That's it.

**PRIYA:** When?

**DANIEL:** I don't know. Three? A little after? I wasn't looking at a clock, I had glue setting.

**MAYA:** Your water bottle's on the workbench. I saw it when I took the recycling out yesterday. The big green one.

**DANIEL:** And it's been sitting in a garage all day. It's warm. I wanted cold water. *(He points at the sink.)* That's my glass, right there.

*(There is a glass upside down in the drying rack. HELEN looks at it, then down at the counter between the sink and the jar.)*

**HELEN:** And this is your sawdust.

*(A fine pale dusting on the counter, between the sink and the owl. HELEN picks up the stack of wax-paper squares. A few pale grains slide off the top one.)*

**HELEN:** On my papers.

**DANIEL:** I was at the *sink*, Mom. Which is right next to the jar, which is right next to your papers. Look at me. *(He holds out his arms; sawdust everywhere.)* I'm leaving sawdust on things I walk past.

**PRIYA:** *(peering at him)* Are you chewing gum?

**DANIEL:** Yes, I'm chewing gum.

**PRIYA:** You chew gum after you've had something.

**DANIEL:** I chew gum when I'm sanding because otherwise I grind my teeth, which you also know, because you're the one who told me I grind my teeth. *(beat)* Six weeks, Priya. I've done six weeks. I'm not going to blow it on one cookie.

**PRIYA:** *(conceding, mostly)* Your numbers were good Thursday.

**DANIEL:** *Thank* you.

*(HELEN turns to MAYA.)*

**HELEN:** And you?

**MAYA:** I was upstairs. Doing my proofs.

**HELEN:** The whole time?

**MAYA:** I came down once. There was a package on the back porch, I saw it from my window. I brought it in.

*(She points. On the bench by the screen door, a small cardboard box.)*

**PRIYA:** That's my vitamins. That came this morning. It's been out there since ten.

**MAYA:** Okay, well, I noticed it at, like, three-thirty, and I brought it in. That's a nice thing I did.

**HELEN:** *(crossing to the screen door, pushing it; it swings loose)* And you didn't latch this.

**MAYA:** It doesn't latch unless you slam it, and you yell at me when I slam it.

**HELEN:** *(coming back to the jar, holding up the lid)* And you didn't press this until it clicked.

**MAYA:** I didn't *touch* that.

**PRIYA:** *(gently)* Honey, you never press anything until it clicks.

**DANIEL:** Neither did Dad. Neither do I, probably. Nobody presses until it clicks, Mom. It's a weird owl.

*(MAYA's phone buzzes in her hand. She glances at it and turns it face-down against her leg, fast. HELEN sees.)*

**HELEN:** Who's that?

**MAYA:** Nobody.

**PRIYA:** Maya.

**MAYA:** *(exasperated, flipping it around)* It's Eli. "ok that actually makes sense now ty." Okay? I sent him a picture of how to do number fourteen because he's freaking out about it and couldn't wait till five. That's what it's about. You can read the whole thread if you want, it's literally triangles.

*(She doesn't hand it over. Nobody takes it.)*

**PRIYA:** *(to HELEN, low)* She has been helping him. His mom told me at the mailbox. He went from a D to a C-minus.

**MAYA:** I can *hear* you.

**PRIYA:** It was a compliment.

**MAYA:** And I don't even *like* raisins. Why would I steal a raisin cookie? That's insane.

**DANIEL:** *(to PRIYA)* And why would I steal one and then *wrap* it?

**PRIYA:** So you could take it out to the garage where I can't see you eat it.

**DANIEL:** Wow.

**MAYA:** *(to DANIEL)* So you could take it out to the garage where Mom can't see you eat it.

**DANIEL:** *(to MAYA)* So you could take it next door.

**MAYA:** He's coming over at *five*. If I wanted to give Eli a cookie I'd ask Grandma for one in front of everybody.

*(Silence. DANIEL looks at the floor. PRIYA looks between them.)*

**PRIYA:** Did you two see each other? When you came in?

**DANIEL:** No.

**MAYA:** No.

**PRIYA:** Who came in first?

**DANIEL:** I don't know when she came in.

**MAYA:** I don't know when he came in. I had headphones on.

**PRIYA:** *(to herself, trying to reconstruct)* I heard the garage door. I definitely heard the garage door. And the screen door, I think. Anjali was crying about the house, I wasn't... *(beat)* I couldn't tell you which one was first. I'm sorry, Helen.

*(Long pause. HELEN looks at the row of thirty-five cookies on the counter. She picks up one wax-paper square and smooths it flat with her palm.)*

**HELEN:** Walt used to take one. Every single time. Every batch for forty-six years, and he always said it wasn't him. *(beat)* I always knew it was him.

**DANIEL:** *(softly)* Mom...

**HELEN:** Eleven bags of three and one bag of two. Father Okonkwo can do the arithmetic. *(She starts laying cookies on wax-paper squares.)* Go on. All of you. Go on.

*(DANIEL hesitates, then exits to the garage. The door sticks, then thunks shut. MAYA hesitates, then exits through the archway. Her footsteps go up the stairs. PRIYA stays a moment, then follows MAYA out.)*

*(HELEN works alone. When the last cookie is bagged, she picks up the empty owl, sets the lid on it, and presses down until it clicks.)*

*(Lights down.)*

**END OF PLAY**
